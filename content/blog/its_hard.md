---
title: Life with LLMs is hard
description: are we becoming stupid?
date: 2026-11-24
---

Having LLMs available to you on a daily basis seems both like a blessing and a curse to me. Sometimes I feel like I can
achieve and learn so much using them - treating it as a personal tutor, looking up stuff much faster on the internet than Google (granted there are sources to check the information from).
Generally doing *useful* things with them while using your brain and aiding your thinking.

On the other hand you can also outsource your through process to the LLM entirely - that thing is scary. Personally, I've been a victim of this as well.
First, when the LLMs were a new shiny thing (roughly 2022-23), it was like magic, so I got hooked and next thing I know - I can't code in 2 months.
Reversing this impact takes quite some time, you really need to go back and actively think about the things again, and it takes effort (and a lot of DSA problems).
Many people don't make this effort and end up in a complete ecosystem with 10 different LLM tools instead. I even ended up asking Mr. ChatGPT for life advice
during that time which now reflecting was very stupid. I might be lucky that I had gone through this phase in the very beginnings of this magical technology before it became globally popular.

For a long time after such experience I was a huge skeptic of AI in general and their usage. I tried doing things my own way, using it as a better Google search via Perplexity only.
But recently, these tools became so much better due to the infinite funding of big tech companies (thanks Jensen, now I have to pay 3k EUR for a usable laptop) that it makes no sense not to use them.
Proving Riemann Hypothesis with Claude's 17 millions line of Lean code is very cool (seriously, good job!), but I see the same pattern that I had seen before with my usage.
We have something that works, it works as a blackbox and we can't explain why it works the way it does. The thought process is completely gone.
We can maybe deduce something from the final "solution", but we don't have the entire process of getting to that solution. While we can plausibly verify the output of an LLM,
say by Lean, writing unit tests, integration tests or whatever, we can't get to that series of decisions which lead to the final result and I find this disturbing.

So now you have this ultra powerful tool which is backed by billions of dollars which can make things faster, probably better and maybe even cheaper than you short-term. But in the long run,
it's the process which matters to me more. Personally, I would like to keep the brain to myself (sorry Anthropic) and even struggle for a little while than to one-shot everything with an agent.
But ultimately, some things I can't do myself, and it's a tool like any other - a very dangerous tool which you should be very careful about. We really need to find a good balance
of not giving up the advantages such tools give us while not giving up your own brain and still being a part of the process, plus let's be honest: screw Anthropic, OpenAI and Google.
You're really better off with a self-hosted LLM which might be a bit worse performance-wise, but still good enough while not giving money to these "nice" companies.

So...Let's think more?
